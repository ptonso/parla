# A job owns the diff baseline

**Affects**: [jobs](../jobs.md), [latex engine](../latex-engine.md), [cli](../cli.md).

## Situation

texfy-ai kept one checkpoint per draft run, and `--revert` restored it. The new work happens in jobs: many rounds of feedback on one part, in one context. The author reviews the diff PDF after each round, and wants to undo a whole job when it went the wrong way. The asset store is not under git.

## Alternatives considered

- **Diff against git.** The asset store is not a repository, so there is nothing to diff against.
- **Keep every job's snapshot.** It costs disk and gives history nobody asked for. The author wants only the last.
- **A standalone `diff` command.** The author prefers a flag on `build`, so the diff comes with every build in the job.
- **`apply` for subagent patches.** A subagent would return exact-text patches that the main agent applies after review. The snapshot already gives the review (the diff PDF) and the undo (`job revert`), so the subagent edits files directly and `apply` ends.

## Decision

`job start` snapshots the artifact and the snapshot is the diff baseline for every build in the job ([jobs](../jobs.md)). `job revert` restores it. Only the last snapshot of an artifact is kept. A job id is the UTC start time. The brief and notes are files the agent writes and `parla` never reads.

## What tipped it

The snapshot serves two needs with one copy: the diff the author reads and the undo the author may want.

## Trade-offs accepted

- A job started late misses earlier changes in its diff.
- Reverting loses everything done since the snapshot, with no partial undo.
- A subagent can change files before anyone reviews them; the diff and revert are the safety.
