## brief

A diagram explains one system at a glance: its parts, how they connect and what travels between them. It earns its place when a reader who skips the text still grasps the mechanism. The author supplies the stages, the labels and the numbers, and you choose the layout and draw it exactly.

The reader looks at the figure first, for seconds, to decide whether the text is worth reading.

Geometry decides whether a diagram reads. Place every element on an explicit grid, keep text inside its box with clear padding, let no shape or arrow overlap another, align edges, space objects evenly, and balance the whitespace so no region is crowded or empty. `parla check` reports overlap and overflow, and the rendered PNG is the final judge, so look at it.
