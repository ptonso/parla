## brief

A talk is an argument delivered in a fixed time to an audience that cannot reread. The slides carry the claim of each moment and its visual evidence, and the speaker carries the explanation. A good deck has one story an audience member can retell afterwards, one claim per slide with its evidence beside it, and a pace the slot can bear.

The audience follows in real time and gives each slide seconds. The usual arc (problem, goal and scope, method, results, summary, references, appendix) is a starting point, and the author's own order replaces it.

## parts

| part | job | topics |
|---|---|---|
| opening | The problem, the goal and the scope. The audience learns why to care and what they will leave with. | deck, claims, summary, selection |
| evidence | The method and results. Each slide has one claim, the figure or table behind it, and a line reading it. | deck, figures, captions, tables, claims, bullets |
| closing | A synthesis of the central claim and what the evidence established, then references, then an appendix holding the long tail for questions. | deck, claims, summary |
