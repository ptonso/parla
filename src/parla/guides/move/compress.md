Goal: say what the passage says in fewer words, with the argument intact.

State each point once, in the shortest form that keeps the claim. Merge sentences that share a subject, put a quantity beside what it measures, and write the action as the verb of the sentence. Where several evidentials chain, keep one clause at the weakest modality the chain contains. Open each paragraph on its claim, and merge a thin paragraph into the one whose goal it shares.

## hard

For a budget the plain form cannot reach. Write less, and stop as soon as the passage fits: drop framing restated from elsewhere first, then the justification of a claim already stated outright, then worked detail no result depends on, then confirmatory numbers that agree with a headline number. Cut whole points, with their transitions. Keep every citation, every quantity a surviving claim rests on and every figure or table reference. When the budget cannot be met without cutting these, stop at the shortest honest version and say so.

## distill

For a part that must carry only its argument. Decide first which beats the part needs (positioning, contribution, methodology, results, discussion), keep the most direct passage for each, and cut the rest whole before writing the survivors at full clarity. A shorter list of fully stated points reads better than a full list of thinned ones.
