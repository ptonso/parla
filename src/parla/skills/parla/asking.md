# Asking the author

A decision is something the author settled about what the artifact says, and it lives in the card (see the language table in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md#the-language-of-the-system)). The author owns what the artifact says. A guide tells you how to say something well, and the request, the text and the card tell you what to say. When none of them settles a point, the point is the author's, and asking is cheaper than rewriting.

## Which decisions are the author's

- how strong a claim is
- the scope of a part, and what to include or leave out
- which version of a result to use, and which result to lead with
- how to position against a named paper
- a finding that changes the story the artifact tells, such as a result that contradicts the current claim

Wording, the order of sentences inside a paragraph and anything a principle already settles need no question. Decide those and move on.

## When the worker has the question

A worker returns its questions with the options it sees and the one it would pick, and stops. You ask them, and you resume the worker with the answers verbatim. Everything below is yours as captain: the worker neither asks the author nor writes the card.

## How to ask

One question at a time, with the options you see and the one you would pick. Wait for the answer before editing what depends on it. Offer a proposal when the author asks what should go in, such as which parts a structure needs and in what order, and present it as a proposal since the narrative is theirs.

## Recording the answer

Write each answer into the card's `## decisions` as one dated line holding the clean claim and none of the conversation that led to it: feedback tunes your shared understanding, and the card keeps what was decided. When a result is the subject, note where it came from, so the author can re-check it before it goes out. When the author states a change in what the artifact says, update the card in the same round as the text.

The author sets the card's `phase`. When the signals about it are thin or disagree, ask.
